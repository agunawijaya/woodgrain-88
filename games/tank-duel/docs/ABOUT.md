# Tank Duel — About

## The hook

Two painted tanks on a table of sandbags and ruins. You fire, and the shell skims a wall, kicks off
the next one with a rising ping, and catches your rival round the corner they thought was safe. The
fireball lights the table, their tank spins wildly, and your own barrel twitches from the recoil.
One more round: this time they are curling a guided shell round the concrete at you, and you have
two seconds to get out of its way. Tanks, then biplanes in the clouds, then jets: 27 duels on one
cartridge, and a Kid on the other stick who plays like a person.

## Inspired by

Inspired by the 1977 tank duel, slot 21 on the 1988 cartridge.

It was a two-player game through and through. A single cartridge held 27 variations behind one
SELECT switch:

- tanks with guided or straight shells, on open ground or in mazes;
- shells that bounce off the walls;
- billiard rules, where only a banked shot counts;
- invisible tanks that show themselves only when they fire or bump into something;
- biplanes and jets duelling over the clouds, a giant bomber against three biplanes, and one jet
  against three.

**There was no computer opponent.** Both vehicles only ever read the two controllers; play it alone
and the other tank simply sat still. Tank Duel keeps every variation and finally gives you someone
to play against.

The 32-in-1 cartridge wired the biplane's stick differently from the original: up and down turned
the plane, left and right set its speed. Tank Duel's _Cartridge wiring_ setting flies it that way.

## What is new

- **The Neighbourhood Kids** as rivals, each with a style of their own:
  - Dee rushes in;
  - Moss waits behind cover;
  - Pip is wonderfully unpredictable;
  - Grandpa Lou banks shells off two walls;
  - Rosa baits you with curling shots;
  - Benny learns your habits.

  They see only what you see and steer with the same stick.

- **A living tabletop diorama.**
  - Painted miniature tanks with turning treads and antennas.
  - Biplanes with spinning propellers, and jets with afterburners.
  - Track marks in the sand and scorch marks where tanks went up.
  - Smoke trails behind guided shells, and sparks on every bounce.
- **Four Remix channels** to earn: Ricochet Royale, Blackout, Ace Duel and Last Tank.
- **The best hit replayed** on the results screen, bank path traced.
- **A tutorial you play**, and records against every Kid.
- **First to 10** beside the original timed round, plus daily runs, challenge links and patches.

## Who it is for

- **Two people on one keyboard or two gamepads.** This is the couch duel it always was; Versus
  needs nothing else.
- **Solo players** get a rival who fights back. Pip is a fair first opponent; Grandpa Lou is a
  long-term project.
- **Newcomers** can play the 90-second tutorial, or turn on Practice, where the other tank sits
  still as it did in 1977.
- **Experts** have billiard channels, invisible mazes and the jet channels, and Grandpa's Respect
  to earn on switch A.

## Light and dark

- **By day**, the table sits in warm afternoon light: low sun, long soft shadows, sand and brick
  in golden tones, and enamel score plates like painted tin.
- **By night**, the room is dark but for a desk lamp over the table and moonlight through the
  window. Tanks run with headlights, tracers glow, and every muzzle flash and explosion lights the
  board around it. The score plates glow from behind like a scoreboard.

Both are designed scenes, not one inverted from the other.
