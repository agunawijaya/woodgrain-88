# Hot & Cold — About

_Find the hidden flag. Every square you check says warmer or colder._

## The hook

Somewhere on a field of 63 squares a flag is buried. You walk your explorer to a square and check
it, and it answers:

- a **number** says how many steps away the flag is;
- an **arrow** says which way it lies;
- a **bomb** blows you back to your home corner.

Two clues together already narrow it down: where the ring of one number crosses the wedge of one
arrow, the flag must be. Race a friend to it, take turns checking, or hunt alone against the
clock. The best moment is the last check before the capture, when you are almost sure.

## Inspired by

Inspired by the hidden-flag hunt, slot 5 on the 1988 cartridge. The original is from 1978, and it
is stranger than it first looks.

- **Clues that vanish.** In 1978 a clue showed only while the button was held down. Let go, and the
  square went blank again. Everything you learned, you had to remember. That is our **Classic
  Memory** mode. **Notebook** keeps every clue on the field for anyone who would rather reason than
  remember.
- **Ten variations from a handful of switches.** Race, turns, hunt for hunt and the clock, each
  with a flag that stands still, bounces off the edges or wraps round them. The written
  descriptions of the cartridge got six of the ten wrong: the settings that choose a variation sit
  in a different order from the one you would guess. We read the program itself to get them right
  (NOTES.md has the table).
- **The 75th check.** In hunt for hunt every check counts, and the game ends when a total reaches 75. Older descriptions read the rule that follows as a secret bonus round for a perfect 75th
  check. It is really a matter of fairness: when the first seat reaches 75, the second seat gets
  one last hunt, so both have had the same number of turns. Our secret patch, **Lucky 75**, keeps a
  little of the legend: find the flag with exactly your 75th check.
- **Quirks we kept.**
  - A flag on a bomb square is captured safely, because the flag is checked first.
  - A bombed explorer stays where it is if the other one is standing on its home corner.
  - The third turns game plays to 75 captures. It is a marathon, faithfully.

## What is new

- **Three looks**, each with a day and a night version:
  - **Polar Expedition**: explorers crunch across a snowfield, and every clue chimes.
  - **Tabletop Board**: wooden pawns on a board game that knocks and clicks like wood.
  - **Radar Room**: a glowing scope where every check pings.
- **Neighbourhood Kids** who hunt the way people do. Grandpa Lou remembers nearly everything; Pip
  forgets half and mixes up the rest. After a match, "What Lou remembered" shows what the Kid kept,
  lost, mixed up or never noticed.
- **The possibility fog**, an optional help that shades the squares the clues have already ruled
  out. It is off in the daily, in challenge links, in the Power Cycle Run and on Versus Night, and
  a run with it on never becomes a record.
- **Four Remix channels**, opened by patches:
  - **Two Flags**: two flags at once, and every clue points to the nearer one.
  - **Minefield**: more bombs, fewer arrows.
  - **Thin Ice**: Notebook clues melt away after ten seconds.
  - **Drift Storm**: the flag moves two squares after every miss.
- **Mouse play**: click a square to walk there, click again to check it.
- **Den Life**: patches, the daily hunt, challenge links, micro-challenges and records.

## Who it is for

Anyone who likes a puzzle with a clock running. Alone, the clock games and the daily are a quiet
deduction game. Two people on one keyboard turn it into a race, where knowing when to stop
checking and start walking wins. Against the Kids, Classic Memory is a test of your own memory as
much as theirs.

## Light and dark

Each look is designed twice: once for the den by day, with sunlight on the snow, the table and the
console, and once for the den at night, with the lamps low and the clues glowing.
